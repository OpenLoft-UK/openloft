Can you board over loft insulation?

Yes, a loft can often be boarded for storage—but the boards should not compress the insulation. A raised platform is usually the practical answer.

Do not lay boards directly onto deep loft insulation and force it down to joist level. Compressing the insulation reduces the air trapped within it and can undermine its thermal performance.

UK government-backed guidance on using a loft for storage describes raising the floor level so insulation can remain at the appropriate depth. The principle is simple: the storage deck belongs above the insulation, not through it.

A raised platform does not make a loft habitable. It creates access and light household storage space only.

How does raised loft boarding work?

A supporting system is fixed above the existing ceiling joists, creating clearance for insulation below. Structural flooring boards are then fitted to that raised support rather than resting on the insulation itself.

The exact detail depends on the joist layout, trusses, insulation depth and obstructions. This is why measuring only the visible floor area is not enough to design the job.

What happens to the insulation?

Existing insulation should remain evenly distributed and should not block intended ventilation routes. If the insulation is damaged, missing or badly laid, deal with that before covering the area with a storage platform.

What about cables and pipes?

Services need to remain identifiable and accessible. The installer should consider cable routes, junctions, pipework, tanks and controls rather than permanently concealing them beneath an uninterrupted floor.

What should be checked before boarding?

  • Joist sizes, spacing and visible condition.
  • Roof-truss configuration and usable headroom.
  • Existing insulation depth and condition.
  • Ventilation paths at the eaves and elsewhere.
  • Water tanks, pipes, cables and other services.
  • Hatch position and a safe route onto the platform.

Official UK insulation guidance also highlights the need to consider tanks, pipes and loft hatches when insulating roof spaces.

What can go on a boarded loft?

Think boxed clothes, decorations, suitcases and other light household belongings. A storage platform is not automatically suitable for dense archives, gym equipment, building materials or very heavy furniture.

The ceiling joists below were not necessarily designed for significant extra load. The Planning Portal’s storage guidance warns that excessive storage loads can exceed the capacity of existing joists. If there is doubt, seek appropriate structural advice.
